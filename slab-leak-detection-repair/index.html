The Problem

The Most Common Hidden Leak In Murrieta Homes

A slab leak is a leak in the water lines running underneath a home's concrete foundation, and it's the single most common hidden leak we diagnose in Murrieta. The signs are often subtle at first, a warm spot, a bill that doesn't add up, before becoming obvious. Catching it early with real detection equipment, instead of waiting for visible damage, is what keeps a slab leak from turning into a much bigger repair.

Signs To Watch For

Signs You Have A Slab Leak

A Warm Spot On The Floor

A patch of flooring noticeably warmer than the rest of the room, especially over a hot water line, is one of the clearest slab leak signs.

Water Bill Spike With No Clear Cause

A bill that jumps with no new usage to explain it often means water is escaping under the slab, out of sight.

Sound Of Running Water With Everything Off

Hearing water movement when every fixture is shut off is a strong sign of an active leak somewhere in the system.

Cracks In Flooring Or Baseboards

New cracks in tile, flooring, or along baseboards can be caused by moisture and pressure changes from water escaping under the foundation.

How We Find It

Non-Invasive Detection Before Any Cutting

We use electronic listening equipment to pick up the acoustic signature of water escaping under pressure, and thermal imaging to spot the heat signature of a hot water line leak through the slab surface. A pressure isolation test at the meter confirms whether the leak is actually under the slab versus elsewhere in the system. This combination narrows the leak to a specific area before we ever break concrete, and any repair follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted statewide in California, which keeps the repair access point as small as possible.

Local Cause

Why Murrieta Has So Many Slab Leaks

Murrieta sits on expansive clay soil that swells during wet winters and shrinks back through the long dry summer. That seasonal movement puts real, repeated mechanical stress on copper lines buried under slab-on-grade foundations. A large share of the city's housing stock was built during the 1990-2006 boom, so a lot of that original copper is aging and stress-fatigued at the same time. Hard water adds a second, separate factor: mineral buildup accelerates internal corrosion, which is a different mechanism from soil-driven joint stress but shows up in the same aging pipe.

Repair Options

Spot Repair vs. Reroute

Spot Repair

  • Access the exact leak point through the slab
  • Lower cost for a single, isolated leak
  • Fastest option when the leak is clearly localized
  • Doesn't address other aging pipe elsewhere

Reroute Above The Slab

  • New line run through walls or attic instead of under slab
  • Avoids cutting concrete entirely
  • Better option after a second slab leak on the same system
  • Higher upfront cost than a single spot repair

FAQs

Common Questions

How do I know if it's really a slab leak and not something else?

A warm spot on the floor over a hot water line, a water bill spike with no explanation, or hearing running water with everything off are the clearest signs. Detection equipment confirms it either way.

Will you have to break up my whole floor?

No. Our detection equipment narrows the leak to a specific area first, so the access point is as small as possible.

Is a spot repair or a full reroute the better option?

It depends on whether this is a first, isolated leak or a repeat issue. We'll give you an honest recommendation, not just the more expensive option.

Does homeowner's insurance cover slab leak repair?

Sometimes, for the sudden water damage a slab leak causes, but usually not the underlying repair itself. Check your specific policy.

How fast does a slab leak need to be addressed?

Sooner is always better. The longer water sits under a slab, the more it can affect the foundation and flooring above it.

Do you serve areas outside Murrieta?

Yes, including Temecula, Wildomar, Lake Elsinore, Menifee, and Canyon Lake.
