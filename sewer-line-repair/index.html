The Problem

When It's The Line Itself, Not Just A Clog

Not every backup is a simple clog. When the same symptoms keep coming back, or a soggy patch or sinkhole starts forming in the yard, the sewer lateral itself may have cracked, separated at a joint, or collapsed under root pressure. That calls for a real repair to the pipe, not another round of snaking that only buys a few more weeks.

Signs To Watch For

Signs Your Sewer Line Needs Repair

Sewage Backup Into The Home

Backup at the lowest drain in the house, often a shower or floor drain, is the most serious sign something is wrong with the line itself.

Sinkhole Or Soggy Patch In The Yard

A soft or sunken area over the line's path usually means the pipe underneath has failed structurally.

Multiple Slow Or Gurgling Drains

Several fixtures acting up together, especially the lowest ones in the house, points past a simple clog toward the main line.

Sewer Odor Outside The Home

A persistent smell near the yard, not just inside, often means a break below ground rather than a clog.

How We Find It

Camera Diagnosis Before Any Digging

Every sewer line repair starts with a camera inspection to see the actual condition of the pipe: where the damage is, how bad it is, and what material we're dealing with. Repair methods follow the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), adopted statewide in California. Together, that tells us honestly whether a localized spot repair will hold, whether trenchless lining or bursting makes sense for a longer damaged section, or whether full excavation is genuinely the only option. We'd rather tell you a spot repair will work than sell a bigger job than the pipe actually needs.

Before We Arrive

What To Do Before We Arrive

If you suspect a sewer line problem rather than a simple clog:

Limit Water Use Until Inspected

Cut back on laundry, dishwashing, and long showers until we've confirmed what's going on, to avoid pushing a backup further.

Keep Kids And Pets Away From Backup Areas

A sewage backup is a real health hazard, not just an inconvenience. Keep the area closed off until it's cleaned and repaired.

Local Cause

Why Sewer Laterals Fail On The Same Timeline As Everything Else

The same 1990-2006 construction wave that produced most of Murrieta's housing stock also laid down the sewer laterals connecting each home to the city main. Many of those laterals are ABS or clay pipe rather than the more root-resistant PVC used in newer construction. Combined with expansive clay soil that shifts with the seasons, the joints in those older laterals are exactly where root intrusion and pipe separation tend to start first.

Repair Method

Trenchless Repair vs. Traditional Excavation

Traditional Excavation

  • Full trench dug along the pipe route
  • Longer job, more yard disruption
  • Necessary for severely collapsed sections
  • Most straightforward for shallow, accessible lines

Trenchless Lining Or Bursting

  • One or two access points instead of a full trench
  • Faster turnaround, less landscaping damage
  • Works well for cracks and root intrusion in otherwise sound pipe
  • Not always possible on severely collapsed sections

FAQs

Common Questions

How do you know if I need a repair instead of just cleaning?

A camera inspection confirms it. If snaking keeps solving the symptom but the same backup returns, that's usually the line itself, not the clog.

Will you have to dig up my whole yard?

Not always. Trenchless lining or bursting needs only one or two access points when the pipe is a candidate for it. A camera inspection tells us which situation we're in.

Is trenchless repair always possible?

No. Severely collapsed or badly offset sections sometimes still need traditional excavation. We'll tell you honestly which one applies.

Does insurance cover sewer line repair?

Usually not for gradual damage like root intrusion, though sudden collapse is sometimes covered. Check your specific policy before assuming either way.

How long does a sewer line repair take?

A spot repair is often a one-day job. Trenchless lining typically takes one to two days, and full excavation can run longer depending on length and access.

Do you serve areas outside Murrieta?

Yes, including Temecula, Wildomar, Lake Elsinore, Menifee, and Canyon Lake.
