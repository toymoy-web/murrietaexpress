The Problem

When Repair Isn't The Right Answer Anymore

Every water heater reaches a point where another repair just delays the inevitable. If yours is leaking from the tank itself, old enough that parts are getting harder to source, or simply can't keep up with the household anymore, replacement is usually the better value even though it costs more upfront than one more repair visit.

Signs To Watch For

Signs It's Time To Replace, Not Repair

Tank Itself Is Leaking

Moisture coming from the tank body, not a loose fitting, means the tank has failed and repair isn't an option anymore.

Repeated Repairs In A Short Window

A second or third repair call within a year or two on a unit over 8 years old usually costs more over time than one replacement.

Household Outgrew The Tank

A growing household running out of hot water mid-shower is a sizing problem, not something a repair fixes.

Rising Energy Bills From An Aging Unit

Water heaters lose efficiency as they age, and Murrieta's hard water speeds that up by coating heating elements in scale.

How We Find It

How We Size And Install

We size the new unit to your actual household rather than defaulting to whatever was there before, accounting for the number of bathrooms, peak usage times, and whether a tankless unit's continuous output genuinely fits your usage pattern better than a bigger tank would. Installation includes removing and hauling away the old unit and setting the new one to current California Plumbing Code requirements, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), including an expansion tank where required. We finish with a full pressure and leak test before calling the job done.

Before Install Day

What To Do Before We Arrive

A few things that make installation day faster and safer:

Clear Access To The Unit

A clear path to the garage, closet, or attic location saves real time on install day, especially for a tankless conversion.

Know Your Gas Or Electric Shutoff

Knowing where your gas or electrical shutoff is located matters for both your safety and ours during the swap.

Local Cause

Sizing For Murrieta's Hard Water From Day One

Local water hardness commonly runs 10 to 25 grains per gallon depending on the district. A new water heater installed without accounting for that will scale up just as fast as the one it replaced. We install with hard water in mind, including recommending a water softener pairing when the household doesn't already have one, since it meaningfully extends the life of whatever unit goes in.

Tank vs. Tankless

Which One Actually Fits Your Household

Traditional Tank

  • Lowest upfront installed cost
  • Familiar technology, simpler future repairs
  • Finite hot water per recovery cycle
  • Typically replaced every 8-12 years in hard-water areas

Tankless

  • Continuous hot water, no standby tank losses
  • Smaller footprint, wall-mounted
  • Higher upfront cost, lower monthly operating cost
  • Needs periodic professional descaling here

FAQs

Common Questions

How long does installation take?

Most tank replacements are completed in 2 to 4 hours. A tankless conversion can take longer if new venting or gas line sizing is needed.

Do you haul away the old unit?

Yes, removal and disposal is included in every installation.

Is a permit required?

Yes, water heater replacement requires a permit in Murrieta. We handle the paperwork and inspection scheduling.

Should I switch from tank to tankless?

It depends on your household's hot water usage pattern and budget. We'll give you the honest trade-offs rather than pushing the higher-margin option.

Do I need an expansion tank?

If your home has a pressure regulator or is on a closed plumbing system, common in newer Murrieta construction, current code requires one.

Do you install in areas outside Murrieta?

Yes, including Temecula, Wildomar, Lake Elsinore, Menifee, and Canyon Lake.
