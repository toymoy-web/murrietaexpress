The Problem

When The Problem Is Before The Water Even Reaches The House

Most leak calls turn out to be interior plumbing, but the water line between the meter and the house is a real, separate point of failure. A leak there shows up differently: pressure affects the whole house at once, not one fixture, and the wet spot, if visible, is out in the yard rather than inside.

Signs To Watch For

Signs The Problem Is The Water Line, Not Interior Plumbing

Low Pressure Throughout The Whole House

Pressure trouble affecting every fixture at once, not just one, points upstream to the main line rather than an interior pipe.

Wet Spot Between The Meter And The House

Standing water or a soggy patch along the line's path between the street and the house is a strong sign of a leak there.

Unusually High Water Bill With No Interior Leak Found

If interior leak detection comes up clean but the bill is still high, the leak may be on the service line itself.

Discolored Water From Every Fixture

Rust-colored water from every tap at once, not just one, often points to corrosion in the main line rather than a single interior pipe.

How We Find It

Locating And Fixing The Line

We use the same non-invasive leak detection equipment we use for interior leaks, adapted for the service line's depth and path, to confirm the leak's location before any digging starts. Depending on the line's material and condition, repair options range from a spot repair to a trenchless replacement that avoids digging up the whole yard between the meter and the house, with any replacement work performed to the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted statewide.

Local Cause

Why Murrieta's Older Service Lines Are Common Repair Candidates

Water lines installed during Murrieta's 1990-2006 growth wave are reaching an age where material fatigue and, on some properties, galvanized or older polybutylene lines become real failure points. The same expansive clay soil that stresses interior slab plumbing puts comparable stress on the buried service line, which is why we see main line failures cluster in the same older neighborhoods as our slab leak calls.

Repair Options

Spot Repair vs. Full Line Replacement

Spot Repair

  • Addresses a single, confirmed leak point
  • Lower cost when the rest of the line is sound
  • Faster turnaround for an isolated failure
  • Doesn't address other aging sections of the line

Full Line Replacement

  • Better option after repeated failures on the same line
  • Trenchless methods limit yard disruption
  • Addresses old or problematic pipe material entirely
  • Higher upfront cost than a single spot repair

FAQs

Common Questions

How do I know if it's the main line and not interior plumbing?

Pressure loss affecting the whole house at once, or a wet spot in the yard between the meter and house, are the clearest signs. We can confirm it with detection equipment either way.

Is trenchless replacement an option for a water line?

Often yes, depending on the existing line's path and condition. We'll confirm during the estimate.

What is polybutylene pipe and why does it matter?

It's a plastic pipe material common in some construction from the 1980s-90s that's prone to failure. If your home has it, we'll flag it honestly as a real replacement candidate.

Will my water be shut off during the repair?

Yes, temporarily, since the repair is on the line supplying the whole house. We coordinate timing to minimize disruption.

Is a permit required for water line work?

Yes for a full replacement, and we handle the permit and inspection scheduling as part of the job.

Is this work held to a specific plumbing code?

Yes, all repair and replacement work follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted statewide in California.

Do you serve areas outside Murrieta?

Yes, including Temecula, Wildomar, Lake Elsinore, Menifee, and Canyon Lake.
