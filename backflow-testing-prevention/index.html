The Problem

A Small Device With A Real Public Health Purpose

A backflow prevention device stops contaminated water from being pulled backward into the clean municipal supply during a pressure drop, which is why local water agencies require annual testing on irrigation systems and certain other connections. It's a quick, routine test, but skipping it can mean a compliance notice or, in some cases, water service issues.

Signs To Watch For

When Backflow Testing Applies To You

You Have An Irrigation System

Properties with a lawn or landscape irrigation system typically have a backflow prevention device that requires annual testing under local water agency rules.

You Received A Testing Notice

Water agencies typically send annual notices when a property's device is due for testing, and missing the deadline can affect service.

You're Installing A New Irrigation Or Fire System

New irrigation systems and fire sprinkler connections generally require a backflow device as part of the installation.

Your Device Failed A Prior Test

A device that failed a previous test needs repair or replacement before it can pass and be certified again.

How We Find It

How Testing And Certification Works

We test the device using calibrated gauges to confirm each check valve is holding properly, then submit the certification paperwork to the relevant water agency on your behalf. If a device fails testing, we'll explain the repair or replacement options honestly, since a failed device generally can't just be re-tested without being fixed first.

Before Your Test

What To Do Before Testing Day

A couple of things that make testing day go smoothly:

Clear Access To The Device

Most backflow devices sit near the irrigation valve box or main shutoff. Clearing landscaping or debris around it saves real time.

Have Your Notice On Hand

If your water agency sent a testing notice, having the account or device number ready speeds up submitting the certification afterward.

Local Cause

Backflow Requirements Across Our Service Area

Water agencies across southwest Riverside County, including Rancho California Water District, Eastern Municipal Water District, and Elsinore Valley Municipal Water District, each require annual backflow testing on applicable connections. That requirement flows from the California Waterworks Standards and local cross-connection control programs, though the exact notice process varies by agency. We handle the paperwork for whichever agency serves your property so you don't have to track down the right form yourself. Commercial properties in particular tend to have more devices requiring testing, which overlaps with what we cover under commercial plumbing service.

Device Types

Common Backflow Device Types

Double Check Valve

  • Common on irrigation systems
  • Two independent check valves
  • Lower-hazard applications
  • Annual testing typically required

Reduced Pressure Zone

  • Used on higher-hazard connections
  • Includes a relief valve for extra protection
  • Often required for fire sprinkler systems
  • Annual testing typically required

FAQs

Common Questions

Do I really need annual backflow testing?

If you have an irrigation system, fire sprinkler connection, or received a notice from your water agency, yes, it's typically required.

What happens if my device fails the test?

We'll explain repair or replacement options. A failed device generally needs to be fixed before it can be certified.

Do you handle the paperwork with my water agency?

Yes, we submit the required certification directly to your water agency after a passing test.

How long does a backflow test take?

Most tests take 20 to 30 minutes for a single device.

Do you install backflow devices for new irrigation systems?

Yes, sized and installed to meet your water agency's requirements.

What happens if I just ignore the testing notice?

Water agencies can restrict or shut off service for a property with an overdue required test, so it's worth scheduling before the deadline rather than after a notice becomes a real problem.

Do you serve areas outside Murrieta?

Yes, including Temecula, Wildomar, Lake Elsinore, Menifee, and Canyon Lake.
