The Problem

When The Line Itself Has Reached The End

A spot repair fixes a specific point of damage. Full replacement is the honest answer once camera inspection shows damage in multiple places, or once the pipe material itself, old clay, Orangeburg, or badly deteriorated ABS, is past the point where patching makes sense. We only recommend replacement when the camera footage actually supports it, not as a default upsell from a simpler repair.

Signs To Watch For

Signs You Need Full Replacement, Not A Repair

Multiple Damaged Sections Found By Camera

When a camera inspection shows damage in more than one spot along the line, patching each one individually often costs more than replacing the whole run.

Repeated Backups Despite Repairs

A line that keeps backing up even after a prior spot repair usually means the damage extends further than what was originally fixed.

Severely Collapsed Or Offset Pipe

A pipe that's collapsed or badly offset at a joint generally can't be patched, it needs to be replaced outright.

Line Predates Modern Pipe Materials

Original clay or Orangeburg pipe from a home's initial construction is often past the point where spot repairs make sense.

How We Find It

Trenchless Lining Or Bursting, When The Site Allows It

Trenchless pipe lining installs a new pipe inside the old one through one or two access points, avoiding a full trench dug along the entire route. Pipe bursting works similarly, breaking apart the old pipe while pulling a new one into place. Both options depend on the existing pipe's condition and the site's layout, which is exactly what the camera inspection tells us before we recommend one method over traditional excavation, all performed to the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted statewide in California.

Local Cause

Why Full Replacement Comes Up On Certain Murrieta Homes

Homes built earliest in Murrieta's 1990-2006 growth wave, along with any property with sewer lines predating that wave entirely, are the ones most likely to need full replacement rather than a spot repair. Decades of root intrusion from mature landscaping combined with the original pipe material's real service life means a lateral installed in the early 1990s is now old enough that multiple failure points are a realistic finding, not a worst-case scenario.

Replacement Method

Trenchless vs. Traditional Excavation For A Full Replacement

Traditional Excavation

  • Full trench dug along the entire pipe route
  • Necessary when the pipe path itself has shifted
  • More landscaping and hardscape disruption
  • Straightforward for shallow, easily accessible lines

Trenchless Replacement

  • One or two access points instead of a full trench
  • Faster turnaround, far less yard disruption
  • Works well when the pipe path itself is still sound
  • Not possible on a severely shifted or collapsed route

FAQs

Common Questions

How do I know if I need replacement instead of a repair?

A camera inspection showing damage in multiple locations, or pipe material that's simply past its service life, are the honest indicators. We won't recommend replacement without that evidence.

Is trenchless replacement always an option?

No, it depends on the existing pipe's path and condition. A severely shifted or collapsed line sometimes still needs traditional excavation.

How long does a full sewer line replacement take?

Trenchless replacement is often completed in a day or two. Traditional excavation can take longer depending on length and access.

Will my yard be torn up during replacement?

Trenchless methods limit disruption to one or two access points. Excavation involves a trench along the route, which we restore as part of the job.

Is a permit required for sewer line replacement?

Yes, and we handle the permit and inspection scheduling as part of the project.

Do you serve areas outside Murrieta?

Yes, including Temecula, Wildomar, Lake Elsinore, Menifee, and Canyon Lake.
