Tankless Water Heaters In Hard Water Areas: What The Manufacturer Manuals Don't Always Emphasize

Published September 2026 · Murrieta Express Plumbing

The manual says descale annually. Here's why that instruction matters more in Murrieta than it does in most of the country.

Tankless water heaters are genuinely great appliances, and most manufacturers back that up with long warranties and solid efficiency numbers. But those numbers and warranties are usually calculated assuming reasonable water quality, and Murrieta's water quality is anything but soft. Here's what the fine print in most manufacturer manuals doesn't fully spell out for a homeowner in a hard water area like this one.

Why Tankless Units Are More Exposed Than Tank Units

A traditional tank water heater stores water and heats it gradually, which means mineral deposits settle at the bottom of the tank over time as sediment. A tankless unit works completely differently. Every single gallon of water that passes through the unit runs directly across a heat exchanger, and every one of those gallons carries whatever mineral content your water supply has. In a hard water area, that means the heat exchanger is constantly exposed to scale-forming minerals in a way a tank's sediment layer never quite mimics. If you're weighing a tankless unit against a standard tank water heater for a replacement, this maintenance difference is worth factoring into the decision.

What The Manual Actually Says (And What It Leaves Out)

Most tankless manufacturer manuals do mention descaling, usually recommending it annually in hard water areas. What they often don't spell out clearly is what "hard water area" actually means in practical terms, or how much faster problems develop here compared to a national average. Murrieta's water commonly falls in the 10 to 25 grains per gallon range. That's well past the point most manufacturers have in mind when they write a generic "hard water" warning into a manual meant for homeowners across the entire country.

What Actually Happens Without Regular Descaling

Skipping descaling doesn't cause a sudden failure. It causes a gradual decline that's easy to miss until it's significant. Scale buildup on the heat exchanger reduces heat transfer efficiency, which shows up first as inconsistent water temperature, sometimes running cooler than expected or fluctuating during a shower. Flow rate drops next, since scale narrows the internal passages the water travels through. Eventually, error codes appear on the unit's display, often well before the unit has reached anywhere near its expected service life.

How Descaling Actually Works

The process itself is fairly straightforward for a technician. A descaling solution gets circulated through the unit using its built-in isolation valves, dissolving mineral buildup on the heat exchanger without needing to disassemble the unit. Done annually, it's a routine maintenance visit. Done rarely or never, the buildup can reach a point where descaling alone doesn't fully restore performance, and a technician may find components that need replacement rather than just cleaning. This is exactly the kind of tankless water heater service visit worth scheduling on a consistent schedule rather than waiting for a symptom.

What Manufacturers Don't Always Say About Warranty Claims

Here's the part that catches some homeowners off guard: many tankless manufacturers can deny or limit warranty coverage for scale-related failures if there's no record of regular descaling, particularly in a documented hard water area. That's a real, practical reason to keep some record of maintenance, not just a vague suggestion. If a heat exchanger fails and there's no descaling history to point to, a warranty claim can become a genuinely harder conversation with the manufacturer than it would be with documented annual service.

What A Water Softener Changes For Tankless Owners Specifically

Pairing a tankless unit with a whole-home water softener doesn't eliminate the need for descaling entirely, but it meaningfully extends the interval between necessary service and reduces the severity of buildup between visits. For a household that's already invested in a tankless system for its efficiency and space savings, adding a softener is often the single highest-leverage way to protect that investment over its full expected lifespan.

A Realistic Maintenance Schedule For Murrieta

Given the local water conditions, annual descaling is a reasonable baseline for most Murrieta households with a tankless unit, and it's worth doing on a consistent schedule rather than only when a symptom appears. If you already have a softener installed, some households can reasonably stretch that interval slightly, though it's still worth a technician's professional judgment rather than skipping maintenance entirely based on an assumption that softening alone solves the problem.

What A Descaling Visit Actually Looks Like

A technician connects to the unit's isolation valves, circulates a descaling solution through the heat exchanger for a set period, then flushes the system clean before restoring normal operation. The whole process typically takes under an hour for a straightforward visit with no additional issues found. If the technician notices anything beyond routine scale buildup while inspecting the unit, like a failing component or an error code history worth investigating, that gets flagged separately rather than folded silently into the descaling service itself.

Signs Your Descaling Schedule Has Already Slipped Too Far

If it's been well over a year, or if you genuinely can't remember the last time the unit was serviced, a few signs suggest the buildup may already be beyond what a routine descaling alone will fully fix. Persistent error codes that return shortly after a reset, water temperature that never quite stabilizes even after descaling, and visibly reduced flow at multiple fixtures simultaneously all point toward buildup that's had years, not months, to accumulate. In those cases, a technician may recommend a more thorough inspection alongside the descaling itself, rather than assuming one standard service will fully restore the unit.

Comparing Tankless To Tank Units In This Specific Regard

It's worth being honest that this maintenance requirement is a real trade-off against tankless units' otherwise strong advantages. A tank water heater in the same hard water conditions also suffers from mineral buildup, but that buildup mostly settles harmlessly at the bottom of the tank rather than directly coating the component doing the actual heating. That's part of why tank units can sometimes go longer without dedicated maintenance before problems show up, even though they also eventually need attention. Tankless units trade that relative maintenance-free period for smaller size, continuous hot water, and better long-term efficiency, as long as the maintenance side actually gets done on schedule.

What To Ask Before Buying A Tankless Unit In This Climate

If you're considering a tankless unit for a Murrieta home and haven't installed one yet, it's worth asking your installer directly about the specific manufacturer's warranty language regarding hard water and maintenance documentation. Some brands are more explicit about this than others, and knowing upfront what's required to keep a warranty valid is more useful than discovering it after a failure. It's also worth asking whether a water softener is being recommended alongside the installation, since pairing the two from day one is simpler than retrofitting a softener onto an already-installed unit later.

Common Questions

How do I know if my tankless unit is overdue for descaling?

If it's been a year or more since the last service, or if you're noticing temperature fluctuation or reduced flow, it's likely overdue.

Can skipping descaling void my warranty?

It can, depending on the manufacturer. Some warranties specifically require documented maintenance in hard water areas, so keeping service records is worth doing.

Does a water softener mean I can skip descaling entirely?

No, but it does reduce how quickly scale builds up, which can extend the interval between necessary service visits.
