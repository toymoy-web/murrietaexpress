The Problem

When One More Repair Isn't The Real Answer

A repipe is a bigger decision than a standard repair, and we don't recommend one lightly. But when a home has had multiple unrelated leaks, when water comes out discolored from more than one fixture, or when pressure has dropped sitewide rather than at a single tap, those are signs the pipe itself, not any one fitting, is the actual problem. At that point, repeated spot repairs usually cost more over a few years than one properly planned repipe.

Signs To Watch For

Signs A Repipe Is Worth Considering

More Than One Leak In A Year Or Two

A single leak is a repair. A second or third unrelated leak on the same system is a pattern worth addressing at the source.

Discolored Water Sitewide

Rust-colored water from multiple fixtures, not just one, usually means the pipe itself is corroding from the inside.

Persistently Low Water Pressure

Mineral buildup narrowing the inside diameter of old pipe reduces pressure sitewide, not just at one fixture.

Visible Corrosion On Exposed Pipe

Green or white corrosion on visible copper, or flaking on old galvanized pipe, is a sign of what's likely happening throughout the system.

Second Leak In A Year?

That pattern is worth a real conversation about the pipe itself.

How We Find It

How A Repipe Actually Works

We map the home's existing plumbing first, identifying which walls and floors give the most direct access to minimize drywall and flooring disruption. Work is typically completed in phases so the home has running water each night rather than being fully shut off for the whole project. We handle the permit and inspection process as part of the job, and walk through material options honestly rather than defaulting to whatever's easiest for us to install.

Local Cause

Why Murrieta Homes From The 1990-2006 Wave Are The Most Common Candidates

The bulk of Murrieta's housing stock went in during the 1990-2006 growth wave, and that original copper is reaching the same age across a large share of the city at roughly the same time. Combine that with expansive clay soil stressing joints and hard water accelerating internal corrosion, and homes from that era are exactly where we see the pattern of repeated, unrelated leaks that makes a repipe the more honest recommendation over another one-off repair. A camera run through the sewer line during the same visit, similar to a sewer camera inspection, sometimes reveals the drain side is aging on the same schedule as the supply lines.

Material Options

Copper vs. PEX For A Repipe

Copper

  • Long track record, familiar to any future plumber
  • More resistant to physical damage
  • Higher material and labor cost
  • Can still corrode from the inside over decades

PEX

  • Lower material and labor cost
  • Flexible, fewer joints and fittings needed
  • Resistant to the corrosion that affects copper
  • Not rated for exterior or high-heat exposure without care

FAQs

Common Questions

How long does a whole-home repipe take?

Most homes are completed in 3 to 7 days, done in phases so water isn't shut off overnight.

Will I have to move out during the repipe?

Usually not. Work is phased to keep at least partial water service running through each day.

Is PEX actually as good as copper?

For most residential applications, yes. PEX resists the internal corrosion that affects aging copper, though copper has a longer overall track record.

Do you patch the drywall after a repipe?

We handle the plumbing access points; drywall patching and paint touch-up are typically coordinated separately unless discussed upfront.

Is a permit required for a repipe?

Yes, and we handle the permit and inspection scheduling as part of the job.

Do you serve areas outside Murrieta?

Yes, including Temecula, Wildomar, Lake Elsinore, Menifee, and Canyon Lake.
