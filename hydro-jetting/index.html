The Problem

When The Line Clogs Again A Few Weeks Later

A snake breaks through a clog and pushes it along, which solves the immediate backup but often leaves buildup coating the inside of the pipe. That's why the same line clogs again on a predictable schedule. Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scour the full diameter of the pipe, not just punch a channel through whatever's blocking it, which is why it holds longer on a genuinely dirty line.

Signs To Watch For

Signs Hydro Jetting Is The Right Fix

Snaking Keeps Buying A Few Weeks

If a line clogs again shortly after snaking, the pipe likely has buildup coating the walls that a snake pushes through but doesn't remove.

Grease Buildup In Kitchen Lines

Kitchen drains carrying years of grease down the line develop a coating that narrows the pipe over time, not a single blockage.

Slow Drainage Across Multiple Fixtures

Several drains running slower than they used to, without a single obvious clog, points to buildup along the line itself.

Root Intrusion Confirmed By Camera

Once a camera inspection confirms roots in the line, jetting is often the right way to clear them before any structural repair.

Same Clog, Different Month?

That's usually buildup a snake alone won't fully clear.

How We Find It

How Hydro Jetting Works

We feed a specialized hose down the line fitted with a nozzle that sprays high-pressure water both forward and backward, cutting through grease, scale, and root intrusion while pulling debris back toward the access point. On older or unknown pipe, we run a camera first to confirm the line can handle full-pressure jetting before starting, since jetting isn't the right call on pipe that's already badly deteriorated.

Local Cause

Where Hydro Jetting Comes Up Most In Murrieta

Mature landscaping planted during Murrieta's original 1990-2006 build-out has had two to three decades to develop root systems near sewer laterals, and root intrusion is one of the most common reasons we recommend jetting over a simple snake. Older kitchen lines that have carried grease for years are the other common case, particularly in homes that haven't had a line cleaned since it was installed. When root intrusion is confirmed by camera, jetting is often paired with a full sewer line repair if the roots have already cracked the pipe itself.

Snaking vs. Jetting

Which One Actually Fixes The Problem

Snaking

  • Breaks through and pushes the immediate clog
  • Lower cost for a one-time simple blockage
  • Doesn't remove buildup along the pipe walls
  • Often needed again on the same line later

Hydro Jetting

  • Scours the full diameter of the pipe
  • Removes grease, scale, and root intrusion
  • Holds longer on genuinely dirty lines
  • Not appropriate for severely deteriorated pipe

FAQs

Common Questions

Is hydro jetting safe for older pipes?

Generally yes for pipe in reasonable condition, but we camera-check first on older or unknown pipe before jetting at full pressure.

How is this different from snaking?

Snaking breaks through a clog and pushes it along. Jetting scours the entire inside diameter of the pipe, removing the buildup that causes repeat clogs.

Can jetting clear tree roots?

Yes, jetting is often effective on root intrusion, though a badly compromised pipe may still need structural repair afterward.

How often should a line be jetted for maintenance?

It depends on usage, but a kitchen line with years of grease buildup often benefits from a one-time jetting even without an active clog.

Will jetting damage my pipes?

Not when the pipe is in reasonable condition. That's exactly why we camera-check older or unknown lines before jetting rather than assuming it's safe.
