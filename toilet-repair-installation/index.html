The Problem

A Running Toilet Costs More Than It Seems Like It Should

A toilet that runs for even a few minutes after every flush can waste a meaningful amount of water over a month, and it's one of the cheapest, fastest repairs we do. Most calls turn out to be a worn flapper or fill valve, parts that cost little but get overlooked because the toilet still technically works.

Signs To Watch For

Signs Your Toilet Needs Attention

It Runs Long After Flushing

A toilet that keeps running is almost always a worn flapper or fill valve, a cheap part that wastes real water on your bill until it's replaced.

Weak Or Incomplete Flushes

A flush that doesn't fully clear the bowl can be a clogged trapway, a low water level in the tank, or, on older units, a design that was simply never great to begin with.

Water At The Base

Moisture around the base of the toilet usually means a failed wax ring seal, not the tank or bowl itself.

Rocking Or Unstable Toilet

A toilet that rocks slightly when you sit down often means the wax ring seal is compromised and needs attention before it leaks.

How We Find It

Repair Versus Replacement

For a running or weak-flush toilet, we check the flapper, fill valve, and flush valve first, since those are the parts that wear out fastest and are the cheapest to fix. For a toilet with water at the base, we check whether it's the wax ring seal or a cracked tank or bowl, since only one of those calls for full replacement. On any toilet from before the early 1990s low-flow mandate, we'll also point out honestly when a full replacement pays for itself in water savings faster than repeated small repairs would.

Local Cause

Why Older Murrieta Toilets Wear Out Faster

Murrieta's hard water, commonly 10 to 25 grains per gallon depending on the district, leaves mineral deposits inside fill valves and flapper seats faster than it would in a soft-water area. That buildup is a common, direct cause of toilets that start running intermittently a few years before you'd otherwise expect a repair call. A whole-home water softener is the most direct way to slow that buildup down across every fixture, not just the toilet.

Repair vs. Replace

When Replacement Actually Makes Sense

Usually Worth Repairing

  • Running toilet from a worn flapper or fill valve
  • Weak flush with no cracks in the bowl
  • Water at the base from a failed wax ring
  • Toilet under about 15 years old

Usually Worth Replacing

  • Visible crack in the tank or bowl
  • Pre-1994 model using far more water per flush
  • Repeated repairs on the same unit within a year
  • Outdated rough-in that limits fixture options

FAQs

Common Questions

Why does my toilet keep running a few minutes after every flush?

Almost always a worn flapper or fill valve. It's a cheap, fast repair, and Murrieta's hard water tends to speed up that wear.

Is water at the base of the toilet a big deal?

It's worth fixing promptly. It usually means the wax ring seal has failed, and leaving it can damage the subfloor over time.

Should I replace an old toilet even if it still works?

If it predates the early 1990s low-flow standard, it uses significantly more water per flush than a modern unit, and the water savings can offset the replacement cost over time.

Do you install comfort-height or accessible toilets?

Yes, we carry and install a range of heights and styles, including ADA-compliant comfort-height models.

How long does a toilet installation take?

Most installations, including removal of the old unit, are completed in under two hours.
