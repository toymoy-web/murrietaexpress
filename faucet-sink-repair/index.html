The Problem

A Drip That Seems Small Adds Up

A faucet dripping once every few seconds can waste a meaningful amount of water over a month, and it's one of the fastest, cheapest repairs we do. Most calls turn out to be a worn cartridge or washer, parts that cost little but get put off because the sink still works well enough day to day.

Signs To Watch For

Signs Your Faucet Or Sink Needs Attention

A Persistent Drip

A faucet that drips even when fully closed usually means a worn cartridge, washer, or O-ring, cheap parts that get overlooked because the fixture still technically works.

Leaking Supply Lines Under The Sink

Moisture or corrosion on the supply lines under a sink is often a slow leak that's been building for a while before it's noticed.

Low Flow Or Sputtering Water

Reduced flow or sputtering at a single faucet usually points to mineral buildup inside the aerator or valve, not a whole-house pressure problem.

Loose Or Wobbly Faucet

A faucet that wobbles at the base often has a loose mounting nut, an easy fix before it stresses the supply connections.

How We Find It

Repair Or Replacement, Diagnosed Honestly

For a dripping faucet, we check the cartridge, washer, and O-rings first, since those wear out fastest and are the cheapest to fix. For a leak under the sink, we trace it to the actual supply line, shutoff valve, or drain connection rather than just tightening the first fitting we see. On an older faucet where replacement parts are hard to source, we'll say so honestly rather than charging for a repair that won't hold.

Local Cause

Why Murrieta Faucets Wear Out Faster Than Expected

Hard water, commonly 10 to 25 grains per gallon depending on the district, accelerates mineral buildup inside faucet cartridges and aerators. That buildup is a direct, common cause of faucets here developing drips or reduced flow a few years before you'd expect based on the fixture's age alone. The same hardness is behind a lot of what we see on other fixtures too, which is part of why water softener installs often come up in the same conversation as a faucet repair.

Repair vs. Replace

When A New Faucet Makes More Sense

Usually Worth Repairing

  • Worn cartridge, washer, or O-ring
  • Loose mounting nut
  • Fixture under about 10 years old
  • Replacement parts still available

Usually Worth Replacing

  • Discontinued model with no parts available
  • Corroded or damaged body, not just internals
  • Repeated repairs on the same fixture
  • Wanting a different style or finish anyway

FAQs

Common Questions

Why does my faucet keep dripping after I tighten it?

Tightening the handle doesn't fix a worn cartridge or washer, which is almost always the actual cause. It's a cheap, fast repair once diagnosed correctly.

Is a leak under the sink urgent?

It's worth addressing promptly. Even a slow leak can damage cabinetry and flooring over time if left unaddressed.

Should I replace an old faucet even if it still works?

If parts are getting hard to source or you've had more than one repair on it, replacement is often the better value long-term.

Do you install kitchen and bathroom faucets both?

Yes, including matching new supply lines and shutoff valves when needed.

How long does a faucet installation take?

Most installations, including removal of the old fixture, are completed in under two hours.
