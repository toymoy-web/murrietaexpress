The Problem

Most Disposal Problems Are Simpler Than They Sound

A disposal that hums but won't spin usually just needs the jam cleared, either with the reset button and an Allen wrench trick or, if that doesn't work, by a technician opening it up. Leaks and persistent electrical tripping are more often signs the unit itself has reached the end of its service life, especially on a disposal more than 8 to 10 years old.

Signs To Watch For

Signs Your Disposal Needs Attention

It Hums But Won't Spin

A humming sound with no grinding usually means the flywheel is jammed, often fixable without a full replacement.

Leaking From The Bottom

Water under the sink near the disposal usually means a failed seal, which typically calls for replacement rather than a seal-only repair.

Frequent Tripping Of The Reset Button

A disposal that keeps tripping its internal breaker is working harder than it should, often due to a partial jam or a failing motor.

Unusual Grinding Or Rattling

Loose or damaged impellers sometimes produce a rattling sound distinct from a normal grinding noise.

How We Find It

Repair Or Replacement, Diagnosed Honestly

We check whether the issue is a simple jam, a failed seal, or a motor problem before recommending anything. A jam is usually a same-visit fix. A leaking seal or failing motor on an older unit is often better addressed with replacement, since disposal repair parts and labor can approach the cost of a new unit fairly quickly.

Local Cause

What We See Most In Murrieta Kitchens

Homes from Murrieta's 1990-2006 construction wave often still have their original disposal units, well past the typical 8 to 12 year service life for a mid-range model. Hard water doesn't affect disposals the same direct way it affects water heaters, but mineral buildup around the seal area is still a contributing factor in the leaks we see on older units. A slow drain backing up under the sink can also put extra strain on a disposal, so if clogs keep recurring in the same sink, it may be worth a look at drain cleaning too.

Repair vs. Replace

When Replacement Is The Better Value

Usually Worth Repairing

  • Simple jam with no leak
  • Unit under about 8 years old
  • Loose mounting or minor adjustment
  • First reported issue on the unit

Usually Worth Replacing

  • Leaking from the bottom seal
  • Unit over 8-10 years old
  • Repeated electrical tripping
  • Repair cost approaching a new unit's price

FAQs

Common Questions

My disposal hums but doesn't spin, what's wrong?

Almost always a jam. Try the reset button, and if that doesn't help, the flywheel usually just needs to be freed, a quick fix in most cases.

Is it worth repairing a leaking disposal?

Usually not, if the leak is from the unit's internal seal. At that point replacement is typically the better value than a partial repair.

How long do garbage disposals typically last?

Most last 8 to 12 years with normal use, sometimes less depending on usage patterns.

Can you install a disposal where there wasn't one before?

Yes, including any needed adjustments to the under-sink plumbing and electrical connection.

What size disposal do I actually need?

It depends on your household's cooking habits. We'll recommend based on actual usage, not just the biggest available unit.

Do you serve areas outside Murrieta?

Yes, including Temecula, Wildomar, Lake Elsinore, Menifee, and Canyon Lake.
