The Problem

Gas Work Isn't Optional Territory For Cutting Corners

Gas line work carries real safety stakes, which is why every job we do is pressure tested and permitted properly. Whether it's sizing a new line for an appliance or repairing an existing one, we treat the process the same way every time, no shortcuts, regardless of how small the job looks.

Signs To Watch For

Signs You Need Gas Line Service

A Smell Of Gas

A rotten-egg smell near an appliance or line is not a wait-and-see situation. Leave the area and call your gas utility's emergency line, then call us.

An Appliance Not Getting Enough Gas

A stove, water heater, or furnace that won't reach full output sometimes means the gas line is undersized for the appliance.

Adding A New Gas Appliance

Installing a new range, water heater, or outdoor fire feature often requires a new or resized gas line.

Visible Corrosion On Gas Piping

Corroded or damaged exposed gas piping should be evaluated before it becomes a real safety issue.

How We Find It

Installation, Repair, And Pressure Testing

For a new installation, we size the line to the appliance's actual BTU requirement rather than guessing, then pressure test the completed line before it's ever put into service. For a repair, we locate the actual issue, whether that's a fitting, a section of corroded pipe, or a connection point, and repair it to current code, following the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and applicable gas piping standards adopted statewide in California.

Gas Safety

What To Do If You Smell Gas

Gas line issues are genuinely safety-sensitive, not just a plumbing inconvenience:

Leave The Area Immediately

Do not operate light switches, appliances, or anything that could create a spark. Get everyone out and away from the smell.

Call Your Gas Utility First

From a safe location, call your gas utility's emergency line before calling us. They can remotely shut off service if needed.

Local Cause

Gas Line Work Across Our Service Area

Homes across Murrieta and the surrounding cities we serve range from older properties with original gas piping to newer construction, and appliance additions, like a new range or an outdoor fire feature, are common reasons for gas line work regardless of a home's age. Every job requires a permit from the relevant city, which we handle as part of the work. A new gas line is often part of a larger project too, like adding a new water heater or upgrading a range, which we can scope together in the same visit.

New Line vs. Repair

What Determines The Right Approach

New Line Installation

  • Adding a new appliance or fixture
  • Existing line undersized for demand
  • Requires permit and pressure test
  • Sized to the appliance's BTU rating

Repair

  • Existing line with a specific fault
  • Corroded or damaged section
  • Requires permit and pressure test
  • Repaired to current code standards

FAQs

Common Questions

What should I do if I smell gas?

Leave the area immediately, don't operate any switches or appliances, call your gas utility's emergency line from a safe location, then call us.

Is a permit required for gas line work?

Yes, and we handle the permit and inspection scheduling as part of the job.

Can you size a gas line for a new appliance?

Yes, we size the line to the appliance's actual BTU requirement rather than assuming the existing line is adequate.

Do you pressure test every gas line job?

Yes, every installation and repair is pressure tested before being put into service.

How long does gas line installation take?

Most single-appliance installations are completed in a few hours, longer if a permit inspection needs to be scheduled separately.

Do you serve areas outside Murrieta?

Yes, including Temecula, Wildomar, Lake Elsinore, Menifee, and Canyon Lake.
