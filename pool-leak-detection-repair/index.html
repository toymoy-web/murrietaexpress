The Problem

We Handle The Plumbing, Not The Shell Or Liner

Pool leaks fall into two general categories: structural issues with the pool shell or liner itself, and plumbing issues in the equipment pad connections or buried supply and return lines. We focus specifically on the plumbing side. If detection points to the shell or liner itself, we'll tell you honestly and point you to a pool specialist rather than guessing outside our expertise.

Signs To Watch For

Signs Your Pool Has A Plumbing-Side Leak

Wet Spot Near The Equipment Pad

Standing water or a soggy area around the pump, filter, or heater connections often points to a fitting or line leak at the pad.

Water Loss Even With The Pump Off

If the pool loses water at roughly the same rate whether the pump is running or off, the leak is more likely structural or on the plumb side than equipment-related.

Air In The Pump Basket

Air bubbles in the pump basket or returns can indicate a suction-side leak in the buried plumbing rather than the pool shell itself.

Autofill Running More Than Expected

An autofill valve that's activating far more than usual is a sign more water is leaving the system than normal evaporation would explain.

How We Find It

How We Check The Plumbing Side First

We start at the equipment pad, checking pump, filter, and heater connections for visible or pressure-tested leaks, since pad-area leaks are often the easiest and cheapest to find and fix. If the pad checks out, we can pressure test the buried suction and return lines, with any plumbing repair performed to the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted statewide. That process helps narrow down whether the leak is in the plumbing at all before you invest in a full pool-specific leak detection service for the shell or liner.

Local Cause

Pools Are Common In Murrieta's Master-Planned Neighborhoods

Many homes built during Murrieta's growth as a master-planned community include a backyard pool, and the same expansive clay soil that stresses household slab plumbing can affect buried pool equipment lines in a similar way over time. We work alongside dedicated pool leak detection companies rather than competing with them on shell and liner diagnosis, focusing on the plumbing connections that are genuinely within a plumber's scope.

Where The Leak Actually Is

Plumbing-Side Leak vs. Shell Or Liner Leak

Plumbing-Side (Our Scope)

  • Equipment pad fittings and connections

  • Often the more affordable fix

Shell Or Liner (Pool Specialist)

FAQs

Common Questions

Do you fix pool shell or liner leaks?

No, that's outside a general plumber's scope. We focus on equipment pad connections and buried supply lines, and we'll refer you to a pool specialist if that's what the diagnosis points to.

How do I know if it's a plumbing leak or the pool itself?

Water loss that continues at the same rate with the pump off often points away from plumbing. We check the plumbing side first and give you an honest answer either way.

Can you check the equipment pad without draining the pool?

In most cases yes, since pad connections and fittings can typically be inspected and pressure tested without draining.

Is a leak at the autofill valve serious?

It's worth addressing, since an autofill running more than expected means real water loss even if it isn't dramatic.

Do you work alongside pool companies?

Yes, we handle the plumbing side and are happy to coordinate with a pool company handling shell or liner work.

Do you serve areas outside Murrieta?

Yes, including Temecula, Wildomar, Lake Elsinore, Menifee, and Canyon Lake.
